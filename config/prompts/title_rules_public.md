MovieWeb title rules (PUBLIC PLACEHOLDER: qualitative summary only. The full house rules contain MW performance data and are never committed; at run time they come from the LEGOLAS_TITLE_RULES_B64 GitHub secret and replace this file.)

1. Shape: subject the reader already cares about, then a genre descriptor, then a strong verb, then a concrete payload. Every element is replaceable except the payload.
2. Vagueness is the main failure. Name the thing that happened: the actor, the role, the date, the number, the exit. If the source headline is vague, fix it; never copy the vagueness and never hold back the name or number to create a curiosity gap.
3. Lead with the biggest accurate name (star, then director, then platform, then property). Describe an unknown property with genre plus platform or pedigree instead of its bare title.
4. "Officially" only for a confirmed, on-the-record event, placed before the verb. Never on a report, rumour or "in talks" story: use "Eyed to", "Reportedly" or "In Talks" instead.
5. Slot changes (a recast or replacement) are stronger than additions; say who is replaced.
6. Performance milestones need the actual number. Production milestones (begins filming, wraps) are weak; pair them with something concrete or pass.
7. Do not open with the show or film title in quotes. Do not quote a sentence of speech as the headline. No questions, no em dashes, avoid colon subtitles.
8. Avoid: "Teases", "Admits", "Reveals Why", "Promising Update", "Begins Production", "Wraps Production", "Final Season", "Exclusive", "Mistake".
9. Accuracy overrides traffic: no unearned superlatives, no implied greenlight, no claim the sources do not support.
10. Hard cap 95 characters.
11. If MW has already run the story, the title must carry the new fact, not restate the old one.
12. When the underlying event is soft, say so with keep=0 rather than dressing it up.
